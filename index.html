The Quantum Comb

The sibling of The Comb. Each cell of the paper is now a qubit, and a sheet no longer holds one groove per cell. It holds one complex amplitude for every whole pattern of the cells: 2ⁿ of them. Each comb is a row of two-qubit gates. Every other comb sits one cell further round, so the comb turns as it goes.

Three new rules

The comb never crushes

Every comb is unitary. Total groove energy Σ|a|² stays exactly 1, and there is no ReLU. Any cut can be uncut by running the comb backwards.

No sheet can be copied

An unknown quantum state cannot be cloned, so a real device can't keep every sheet. It has one sheet, cut in place. Only a classical simulator can keep them all.

You see one groove per spin

Reading the sheet returns one pattern, drawn with probability |a|². n qubits give n bits per reading, however many amplitudes were inside.

Schrödinger: keep the sheets––Memory doubles with every qubit. Simulators stall around 50 qubits for this reason.
Feynman: sum the histories, depth first––Memory is tiny and work doubles with every qubit in every layer. This schedule is the proof that quantum computers can be simulated in polynomial space (BQP ⊆ PSPACE): the Regrow button of The Comb, with complex teeth.
The physical comb––Small in space and small in time. The bill moves to the readout: one pattern of n bits per run, drawn at random.

Reversibility replaces erasure here. A classical comb that crushes values has to keep them or regrow them. A quantum comb never crushes, so anything it computed on the side has to be uncomputed by running backwards (Bennett 1989). That's the reversible cousin of Williams' regrowing tree.
